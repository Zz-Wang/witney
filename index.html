Chapter One

The Story

Entry — 16 June

I won Witney at auction on the 16th of June — a 1970 BMW 2000 Neue Klasse, four doors, Chamonix over blue fabric, sitting patiently in a listing out of Norrköping. The photos showed a car that had clearly been loved rather than pampered: the classic Hofmeister kink, the upright grille, and a colour that photographs almost cream in some light and pure white in others.

What sold me wasn't the shine, it was the paperwork. Whoever owned this car before me kept nearly everything — the interim police licence dated back to October 1969, the original instructions for the Blaupunkt radio, the warranty and service booklet, handwritten receipts, and what looks like every inspection protocol the car has ever been given. It's rare to buy a fifty-plus year old car and inherit its entire memory along with it.

Mechanically she's honest rather than perfect: the head was rebuilt five years ago, the water pump replaced last year, and the front brakes gone through with reconditioned calipers and fresh lines. The body was partly repainted a couple of years back from the door sills down, with the panels above said to still be wearing their original paint. There's a small patch of rust by the right rear wing and a dashboard clock that doesn't tell time — small honesties I'd rather know about than not.

Entry — The Drive Home

Getting Witney home meant a trip from Linköping to Göteborg — new territory for a car that, as far as I know, has spent its life closer to the east coast. She rode home on her own four wheels for that first leg, the M10 and its automatic gearbox staying smooth while the Swedish countryside went by outside a windscreen that already carries one (or two) small stone chip earned decades before I ever saw the car.

Now she's parked in Göteborg, and the real work starts: learning her sounds, her quirks, and eventually getting her out on the road under her own power. This page will grow as that happens.
